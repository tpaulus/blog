The culmination of this year's summer vacation brought us to the small town of Baden-Baden in southwest Germany. Neither Melinda, nor I had been this far south in Germany before, but after a trip which started at a friend's wedding in Denmark, we wanted to relax for a few days before going back to the _real world_. Baden-Baden is famous for its spas and architecture spanning the 18th through the 20th centuries. Today, you can visit the older Friedrichsbad and the newer Caracalla Spa (which shared a basement with our hotel). We visited both and their stark differences highlight how the world outside of the spa has changed over the years.

### Friedrichsbad

We visited Friedrichsbad on our first full day in Baden-Baden. Most days of the week, the bath operates in its truest form - without swimwear. This made us somewhat anxious at first, coming from the US where such a thing would be unheard of, but we decided to lean in and make the most of our time here. From the time we checked-in and got out wrist bands, everything was included. We put our cloths and belongings in the provided lockers (which locked and unlocked via the chip in the wristband), wrapped ourselves in the sheet provided and walked down the hallway towards the sounds of water.

An attendant provided us an overview of the stations we would be visiting and provided us with sandals. The first station saw us hanging up our sheets and taking a shower under an unimaginable volume of spring water. From there we went from saunas to steam rooms to pools of various temperatures. We finished up with a final shower under non-spring water and some lotion before settling into a lounge chair with a cup of tea and a selection of magazines to peruse.

Everything was included, there was no time limit. We had read that your mind gets to rest at Friedrichsbad, but we did not really believe it. At the end of our time there, we both believed it and enjoyed it.

> After 10 minutes you forget time, after 20 minutes you forget the world.
> 
> ~ Mark Twain commenting about the Friedrichsbad Spa.

### Caracalla

Two days later we visited the newer Caracalla spa. Built 108 years later it was a completely different environment, despite being modeled after the original Friedrichsbad spa to some extent. Tickets to Caracalla are sold by time allotments (2 hours, 3 hours, or the full day), with overages costing 1€ per 10 minutes. Towels are available to borrow at 6€, and there is of course a restaurant/cafe where one can purchase somewhat overpriced food and drink. Just like at Friedrichsbad outside food items are not allowed, but there are also no drinking water stations across the spa like we saw at Friedrichsbad.

We headed over from our hotel via the parking garage they share and made our way inside, leaving our wallets in the lockers (also lockable via the wristbands that are used at Caracalla) before heading inside. Caracalla requires swimwear to be worn everyday in their pools, but have textile-free (nude) saunas which are available as an add-on purchase. The main attraction of Caracalla are the various pools, although for our visit the indoor pools had been drained for maintenance works. While it did not seem overly crowded, especially since it was mid-morning on a Wednesday in mid-September, it was full. Towels draped over most of the lounge chairs, all of the heating beds occupied.

Each of the pools had been designed to have unique features across the pool, jets in some areas, a waterfall in others, bubble chairs in a different area. As we entered the pool and floated around, we could not help but realize that all of the _good_ spots were taken. A few minutes later we heard the sound of the water change, a waterfall had come on and the jets the middle of the pool turned off. The patrons who had been under the jets swam over to the new focus area. A little while later, the attractions cycled again - the water fall turned off and the jets turned on around the permitter of the pool. Once again, the crowd shifted. As the cycles continued, one thing became increasingly apparent, the number of patrons who wanted to enjoy the attraction outnumbered the amount of patrons the attraction could support. There were only so many jets, so many seats. The cycling made it feel a bit like a game, leaving you waiting to see if you would get a chance - all the while keeping an eye on the clock to avoid incurring overage charges.

### Outside the Pool

While we swam around in Caracalla's pools, or sat in the steam room, I could not help but think back to Friedrichsbad. My mind was the opposite of relaxed, it was engaged. Caracalla felt like a representation of the market-forces I witness at home on a daily basis. Caracalla took the Friedrichsbad and distilled it down to its essence and then scaled it up 100-times. The pools are all significantly bigger, the domes higher and bigger, the ability to extract revenue out of patrons higher. The spirit is lost, as is the story. Bigger is not always better.

Many of us go through the world each day thinking about our outward appearance – the cloths we wear, how we style our hair, so on and so forth. That all goes out the window when you strip-down and take a shower under what can only be described as a never ending bucket. All you are left with is your thoughts and beliefs.

If you want to go swim in giant warm pools filled with water that has alleged healing properties, go to Caracalla. If you want an experience that will bring you inner peace and tranquility, go to Friedrichsbad - preferably on one of the textile-free days.
