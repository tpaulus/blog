The culmination of this year's summer vacation brought us to the small town of Baden-Baden in southwest Germany.
Neither Melinda, nor I had been this far south in Germany before, but after a trip which started at a friend's wedding
in Denmark, we wanted to relax for a few days before going back to the _real world_. Baden-Baden is famous for its spas
and architecture spanning the 18th through the 20th centuries. Today, you can visit the older
[Friedrichsbad](https://friedrichsbad.net/) and the newer [Caracalla](https://caracalla.de/) Spas (the latter shared a
basement/parking garage with our hotel). We visited both and their stark differences highlight how the world outside of
the spa has changed over the years.

### Friedrichsbad

We visited Friedrichsbad on our first full day in Baden-Baden. Most days of the week, the bath operates in its truest
form - without swimwear. This made us somewhat anxious at first, coming from the US where such a thing would be unheard
of, but we decided to lean in and make the most of the experience. From the time we checked-in and got our wrist bands,
everything was included. We put our cloths and belongings in the provided lockers (which locked and unlocked via the
chip in the wristband), wrapped ourselves in the sheet provided and proceeded down the hallway towards the sounds of
flowing water.

The attendant provided us an overview of the stations we would be visiting and provided us with sandals – color coded
by size in true german fashion. The first station had us immediately hang up our sheets and take a shower under an
indescribable volume of spring water. From there we progressed from saunas to steam rooms to pools of various
temperatures. We finished up with a final shower under non-spring water and applied lotion (made using spring water of
course) before settling into a lounge chair with a cup of tea and a selection of magazines to peruse.

Everything was included, there was no time limit. Most of the time there was not even a clock in sight. We had read
that your mind gets to rest at Friedrichsbad, but we did not really believe it. At the end of our time there, we both
believed and enjoyed it.

> After 10 minutes you forget time, after 20 minutes you forget the world.
> 
> ~ Mark Twain commenting about the Friedrichsbad Spa

### Caracalla

Two days later we visited the newer Caracalla spa (108 years newer). Despite being modeled after the original
Friedrichsbad spa to some extent, it was a completely different environment. Tickets to Caracalla are sold by time
allotments (2 hours, 3 hours, or the full day - no re-entry permitted), with overages costing 1€ per 10 minutes. Towels
are available to borrow at 6€, and there is of course a restaurant/cafe where one can purchase somewhat overpriced food
and drink. Just like at Friedrichsbad outside food items are not allowed, but there are also no drinking water stations
across the spa like we saw at Friedrichsbad.

We headed over from our hotel via the shared parking garage and made our way inside, leaving our wallets in the lockers
(also lockable via the wristbands that are used at Caracalla) before heading inside. Caracalla requires swimwear to be
worn everyday in their pools, but have textile-free (nude) saunas which are available as a 5€ add-on purchase. The main
attraction of Caracalla are the various indoor and outdoor pools. Unfortunately during our visit the indoor pools had
been drained for maintenance. While it did not seem overly crowded, especially since it was mid-morning on a weekday in
mid-September, it was full. Towels draped over most of the lounge chairs, all of the heating beds occupied.

Each of the pools had been designed to have unique features across the pool, seats with jets in one areas, a waterfall
in another, water jets in yet a different area. As we entered the pool and floated around in the current, we could not
help but realize that all of the _good_ spots were taken. A few minutes later we heard the sound of the water change, a
waterfall had come on and the jets the middle of the pool turned off. The patrons who had been under the jets waded
over to the new focus area. A little while later, the attractions cycled again - the water fall turned off and the jets
turned on around the permitter of the pool. Once again, the crowd shifted. As the cycles continued one thing became
increasingly apparent, there was just not enough attraction availability to support the patron demand. There were only
so many jets, so many seats. The cycling made it feel a bit like a game, leaving you waiting to see if you would get a
chance - all the while squinting at the clock on the far end of the pool deck to avoid incurring overage charges.

### Outside the Pool

While we swam around in Caracalla's pools, or sat in the steam room, I could not help but think back to Friedrichsbad.
My mind was the opposite of relaxed, it was engaged. Caracalla felt like a representation of the market-forces I
witness at home on a daily basis. Caracalla took Friedrichsbad and distilled it down to its essence and then scaled it
up 100-times. The pools are all significantly bigger, the domes higher and bigger, the ability to extract revenue out
of patrons greater. The spirit is lost, as is the story. Bigger is not always better.

Many of us, myself included, go through the world each day thinking about our outward appearance – the cloths we wear,
how we style our hair, so on and so forth. That all goes out the window when you strip-down and take a shower under
what can only be described as a never ending bucket. All you are left with is your thoughts and beliefs, and in time,
the warm water and steam take your thoughts with them.

If you want to go swim in giant warm pools filled with water that has alleged healing properties, go to Caracalla. If
you want an experience that will bring you inner peace and tranquility, go to Friedrichsbad - preferably on one of the
textile-free days.
